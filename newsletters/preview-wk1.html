Week 1 Preview

Updated Thursday midday with ESPN's numbers: the live score from Wednesday's opener (Seahawks 13, Patriots 10), ESPN's projected total for every lineup, and its win probability for each matchup. The Rams and 49ers play tonight in Melbourne at 8:35 PM ET; everyone else waits for Sunday. The power rankings and schedule strength further down are the preseason model's and don't move until a week is scored.

Injury desk — three big names, one bad 48 hours

Brock Bowers (LV, TE) — Rickhouse ROYalty

The league's TE1 had a meniscus trim on Tuesday in Dallas and is out Sunday against Miami; NFL Network's Ian Rapoport says "a game or two." The injury dates to camp and wasn't diagnosed until last week. Impact: Rickhouse didn't wait around — Michael Mayer, Bowers' backup in Las Vegas, is already on the Rickhouse roster off the wire and projects for 9.3 in the slot Sunday at 4:25 against Miami. A fill-in rather than a replacement, but the fastest waiver claim of the week.

Sam Darnold (SEA, QB) — Tritium Badgers, bench

Sacked by Dre'Mont Jones on Seattle's opening drive Wednesday and gone for the night with a hip injury after one completion for 13 yards. A CT scan ruled out a fracture ("encouraging but inconclusive"), an MRI was scheduled for Thursday, and Rapoport says short-term injured reserve — a minimum four games — is "certainly in play." Drew Lock finished the game 16 of 22 for 187 yards and a touchdown. Impact: Tritium starts Jalen Hurts, so their lineup doesn't change, but Darnold was their trade chip (see below), and a bench QB who may not throw a pass until October is worth exactly what it sounds like. Seattle doesn't play again until September 20.

A.J. Brown (NE, WR) — Green Eggs and Sam

Three catches for 26 yards plus a 34-yard pass-interference draw that set up New England's only touchdown, then a Seahawks defender's knee came down on his right ankle in the third quarter and he left in a walking boot. NFL Media reported Thursday that it's believed to be a high-ankle sprain, with an MRI to set the timeline. Impact: Green Eggs already has the Wednesday line locked in a WR slot — 5.6 points against a 14.2 projection — and can't swap it. If Brown misses time, Romeo Doubs (Road Islanders' bench) and Pop Douglas are next in line for the targets, and Rhamondre Stevenson, who caught five balls Wednesday, becomes an even bigger piece of that offense.

This week's matchups

To Infinity and Bijan! (0-0) at Tritium Badgers (0-0)

ESPN makes it Tritium 116.6 to 111.9, a 52-48 lean to the home side, and nobody in this one has played yet. The Badgers roll out Jalen Hurts (21.1 projected, the biggest number on either side) and a two-headed RB monster in Jonathan Taylor and Travis Etienne, with Trey McBride's 14.4 at tight end the quiet edge; Rome Odunze is Questionable, and Sam Darnold's hip is a bench problem, not a lineup one. Bijan Robinson (19.3) anchors the visitors with Caleb Williams under center, and they get the first real points of this matchup tonight when Kyren Williams takes the field for the Rams in Melbourne. The closest thing to a coin flip that hasn't flipped yet — bring snacks.

Gofffather (0-0) at Road Islanders (0-0)

Your newsletter author's own Road Islanders have already lost the favorite tag: ESPN has Gofffather 118.2 to 116.7 and gives them 51 percent, because the Seahawks D/ST they started produced three sacks, three interceptions and 10 points allowed Wednesday — 14.0 in the bank against a 7.2 projection. Gofffather still has Bo Nix, Chase Brown, Quinshon Judkins and Emeka Egbuka to come, with Sam LaPorta at tight end. The Islanders answer with Jahmyr Gibbs (22.4 projected, the highest single number in the league this week), Jared Goff, Javonte Williams and George Pickens; Jakobi Meyers' thumb (Questionable) is the only tag on the sheet, and their only Wednesday player was bench WR Romeo Doubs, who caught zero of three targets. Fourteen points down before kickoff, and the model shrugs and calls it a two-point game.

Team 3 (0-0) at Captain Chaos (0-0)

ESPN: Captain Chaos 116.5 to 112.1, 52-48, with Chaos already holding 7.0 from kicker Jason Myers (two field goals and an extra point Wednesday). Chaos leans on Puka Nacua (21.0 projected, second-highest in the league), Jaxson Dart and Chris Olave, though Jeremiyah Love is Questionable after his first practice in a month and Isiah Pacheco is on IR. Team 3 counters with the league's best pure talent in Justin Jefferson, Saquon Barkley and Brock Purdy, but Zach Charbonnet is stuck on PUP recovering from ACL surgery. Tonight decides the early lean: Team 3 has Purdy and George Kittle (expected on a snap count) in Melbourne; Chaos has Nacua. Chaos by name, favorite by number — barely.

Team Simmons (0-0) at Motorcity-meh (0-0)

The week's first fireworks belong to the home side: Jaxon Smith-Njigba went 8 catches, 122 yards and a touchdown Wednesday, 26.2 points against an 18.9 projection, and ESPN now has Motorcity-meh at 125.6 — the highest projected total in the league — to Team Simmons' 118.9, a 53-47 favorite. Lamar Jackson (19.1) leads what's left, with Kenneth Walker and David Montgomery in the backfield. Simmons brings CeeDee Lamb, Dak Prescott and Ashton Jeanty (18.3, full practice Wednesday), but Zay Flowers and Jonathon Brooks are both Questionable. Tonight, Simmons has Rams kicker Harrison Mevis and Motorcity has Mike Evans in the 49ers' lineup. Meh matchup on paper; on Wednesday it was the loudest game in the league.

AMONsters Inc. (0-0) at Artificial Win-telligence (0-0)

A genuine 50-50 on ESPN's board: Artificial Win-telligence 113.7 to AMONsters 111.9, with AMONsters holding 7.0 from the Patriots D/ST (one sack, 13 points allowed) and, crucially, Drake Maye's three-interception night parked on their bench — Joe Burrow (18.2 projected) is their starter. Omarion Hampton and Derrick Henry carry the AMONsters backfield, and Terrance Ferguson plays tight end for them tonight in Melbourne. Win-telligence trusts Ja'Marr Chase (20.0, the top receiver projection this week) and Breece Hall while Patrick Mahomes is Questionable coming off ACL surgery; TreVeyon Henderson sat Wednesday, and Deebo Samuel plays tonight for the 49ers. Even money, and the side with the bigger quarterback question is the one laying nothing.

Robots Helping (Themselves) (0-0) at Green Eggs and Sam (0-0)

ESPN: Robots 110.9 to Green Eggs 106.2, 53-47 to the visitors, and Green Eggs' 5.6 on the board is exactly the problem — that's A.J. Brown's 3 catches for 26 yards before the ankle, locked in a WR slot against a 14.2 projection (see the injury desk). The Robots ride Christian McCaffrey (18.4), Jayden Daniels and Drake London, with Malik Nabers Questionable. Green Eggs counter with Justin Herbert (19.0), De'Von Achane and Davante Adams. Tonight is the big one here: Green Eggs has Adams and the Rams D/ST on the field in Melbourne, while the Robots have McCaffrey, Blake Corum and kicker Eddy Pineiro. Three players a side before Sunday, and the WR1 already in a walking boot.

Rickhouse ROYalty (0-0) at The Provid-End Zones (0-0)

The "blowout special" is gone. The Provid-End Zones have 27.9 already banked from Wednesday — Rhamondre Stevenson 14.5 (18 carries for 51, 5 catches for 44), Jadarian Price 7.8 (10 carries for 52) and Hunter Henry 5.6 (3 catches for 26) — and ESPN still calls it Rickhouse 107.5 to Provid-End 107.0, a 51-49 lean to the visitors, because Price and Henry came in under projection and Rickhouse's whole lineup is intact for Sunday. Josh Allen (19.3), Amon-Ra St. Brown and Rashee Rice are still to come for the home side. Rickhouse counters with James Cook, DeVonta Smith and Malik Willis, and Michael Mayer, claimed off the wire after Brock Bowers' meniscus trim, fills the tight end slot against Miami; Alec Pierce is Questionable on the bench. Twenty-eight to nothing and a coin flip. That's Week 1 for you.

Trade of the week — postponed by injury

Thursday morning's version of this proposal — Tritium Badgers send Sam Darnold (228.3 rest-of-season points) to Team Simmons for Michael Wilson (166.0) — was written before Darnold left the opener with a hip injury that may cost him four games on short-term IR. Simmons isn't taking that call this week, and the rest-of-season number is no longer worth quoting. What hasn't changed: Tritium's 288.6-point starter gap at wide receiver is real and Wilson is still the fix. Wait for the MRI, then re-price with a different chip — the Badgers' bench still has J.K. Dobbins and Dalton Kincaid to shop. Sometimes you gotta pay a premium to stop starting a bye-week special at WR3; sometimes the premium gets hurt first.
