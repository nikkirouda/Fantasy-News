Week 1 Recap

Welcome back. Seven games, seven winners, and one theme: the projections took a beating. Five starters in this league beat their number by twenty points or so, and a handful of others missed theirs by enough to decide a lineup's whole afternoon. So this edition introduces the Anchor, awarded each week to the starter who finished furthest below his projection. Two rules. Kickers are excluded, because a kicker's afternoon is the weather and someone else's red-zone habits. And the field is the starters this edition names, top scorers and busts on every side, with the projections taken from the same feed the Thursday preview uses. Nominations run through the results; the verdict is at the bottom.

Results

Motorcity-Meh 175.7 beat Team Simmons 113.3

The week's high score belonged to Kenneth Walker III, who beat his projection by twenty, the largest overshoot by any starter in the league, on a team where everyone else simply did their jobs. Team Simmons got a double-the-forecast day from Ashton Jeanty and it bought them nothing, because the rest of the lineup was ordinary and the other side was not.

Road Islanders 144.0 beat Gofffather 87.2

Your correspondent's Road Islanders got the Jahmyr Gibbs day the draft promised, comfortably clear of his projection, and would prefer not to discuss George Pickens at the flex, who finished nearly ten below his number while Kyle Monangai watched from the bench. That is an Anchor nomination filed against the newsletter's own author, which the committee reports with a straight face. Gofffather's day began and ended with Chase Brown; Courtland Sutton's 3.1 from a starting spot was a nomination too, though the margin here was decided long before either man's shortfall mattered.

Captain Chaos 169.4 beat Team 3 112.8

Captain Chaos looked exactly like the team the model has at the top of the rankings below, with D'Andre Swift more than doubling his projection and nothing else required. Team 3 got a big day from Justin Jefferson, well clear of his forecast, and a negative number from the Browns defense, which is on brand and earns a nomination that changes nothing at this margin. The second-biggest win of the week, and the least dramatic.

The Provid-End Zones 151.9 beat Rickhouse ROYalty 98.6

Josh Allen beat his projection by sixteen and The Provid-End Zones never looked back. Rickhouse ROYalty got a better-than-forecast afternoon from Parker Washington, an Anchor nomination from Tony Pollard at seven under his number, and a loss by more than fifty.

Artificial Win-telligence 126.7 beat AMONsters Inc. 87.6

Artificial Win-telligence won without fuss, Patrick Mahomes clearing his projection, while the Chargers defense finished eight below its number and scored nothing at all, an Anchor nomination in a game that was never close. AMONsters Inc. got the second-largest overshoot of the week from Derrick Henry and lost by thirty-nine anyway, because Terrance Ferguson gave them a blank at tight end.

Tritium Badgers 153.6 beat To Infinity and Bijan! 118.5

Jonathan Taylor set the pace and cleared his number, Rome Odunze fell a little short of his, and the Tritium Badgers never looked troubled. To Infinity and Bijan! had the best individual performance in the whole league, Caleb Williams beating his projection by nineteen for Player of the Week, and lost by thirty-five because Colston Loveland turned a projection of nearly fourteen into a zero. That is the worst miss by any starter this edition tracks, and the Anchor's clear favorite. A team that produces the week's best player and still loses is not a bad team; it is a team with one slot off.

Robots Helping (Themselves) 70.2 beat Green Eggs and Sam 62.2

The closest game of the week was also the lowest-scoring. Robots Helping (Themselves) won with the smallest winning total in the league while their Texans defense finished eleven below its projection at minus four, the second-worst miss of the week and a nomination that, for once, nearly mattered. Jayden Daniels landed almost exactly on his number and was the only starter on either side to do so. Green Eggs and Sam got a quiet day from Justin Herbert and a rounding error from the Rams defense, both short of forecast, in an eight-point loss. Two teams that will be glad the season is long.
