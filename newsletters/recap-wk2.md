# Week 2 Recap

Two weeks in, and the league has found its tempo: seven games, three of them settled by less than a point and a half. Call it Small Change Sunday. Somebody won this week on the other side's missed field goal, somebody else on a defense that scored below zero and still cashed the ticket. The Anchor is back, same rules: the starter who finished furthest below his projection, kickers excluded, verdict at the bottom. This week two of the nominees were on winning teams, which says something about what decides a close game, and it is not the empties.

## Results

### Green Eggs and Sam 136.48 beat Gofffather 89.92

Last week's low scorer posted this week's high score, which is the kind of turnaround that makes a manager insufferable at work. Davante Adams did most of it, nearly forty points on eight catches, with Waddle and a Cowboys tight end behind him having the afternoons their projections did not expect. Gofffather went the other way: projected to win, then Bo Nix and Courtland Sutton came in light from the same Denver game and nobody else picked up the slack. 0-2, and the biggest margin of the week against their name.

### Road Islanders 114.28 beat To Infinity and Bijan! 79.82

Your correspondent's team came in twelve under its projection and won by thirty-four, which tells you everything about the other side. To Infinity and Bijan! were projected to score 121 and produced the league's low total; Caleb Williams left the game hurt, Colston Loveland blanked at tight end for the second straight week, and the kicker was their leading scorer. The Islanders' case was Jared Goff, four touchdowns and a top-ten day among all starters, with a benched Patriots defense the author would rather not discuss and will, in the Anchor section, be made to.

### Rickhouse ROYalty 117.02 beat Artificial Win-telligence 104.68

The projection had this as the widest gap of the week, favoring Artificial Win-telligence, and Rickhouse ROYalty won it starting Carson Wentz. Patrick Mahomes and Ja'Marr Chase both cleared their numbers on the losing side and it did not matter, because Nico Collins was ruled out for a zero and Tucker Kraft caught two balls. Rickhouse got DeVonta Smith nearly doubling his forecast and sixteen from Harrison Butker, and the team ranked last in every model column a week ago is 1-1 with the third-best score of the week.

### Team 3 89.68 beat Robots Helping (Themselves) 86.54

The closest projection on Thursday came in nearly thirty points under on both sides and stayed close anyway. The 49ers offense was split between these two lineups, and it broke for Team 3: Purdy and Kittle outscored McCaffrey and the kicker. Robots got 1.1 from Malik Nabers and 2.5 from Kyle Pitts, and that was the game. Team 3 won it with Saquon Barkley at three points after leaving hurt, and won it anyway because their second-best starter, Kittle, outscored the second-best on the other side, which turns out to be the week's whole story.

### The Provid-End Zones 118.02 beat Motorcity-Meh 117.0

The two best totals in the league, a point apart. The Provid-End Zones started the Lions defense against their own Josh Allen and took minus four from it, then won anyway because Allen and Amon-Ra St. Brown were on the same side of the ledger, seventy-six points between them. Motorcity had the Player of the Week, Jaxon Smith-Njigba with three touchdowns, and lost, because Montgomery, Goedert and a missed Dicker kick were underneath him. First loss for the Week 1 high scorer. Small Change Sunday, entry one.

### Captain Chaos 87.7 beat AMONsters Inc. 87.02

The household derby was decided by sixty-eight hundredths of a point, and the winners did it with three blanks in the starting lineup: Jaxson Dart left the game with under a point, Puka Nacua was out, DJ Moore finished below zero. Kelce and Olave carried the rest. AMONsters had a zero of their own at tight end and eight points from Drake Maye, and will be hearing about it over the toast for exactly one week, when this margin is quietly used as a tiebreaker in every domestic dispute. Small Change Sunday, entry two, and the closest household derby the league will see for a while.

### Team Simmons 114.96 beat Tritium Badgers 114.46

Half a point. The closest game of the season, and Team Simmons won it with a zero at flex, Zay Flowers scratched, and a running back who managed two points. Dak Prescott and CeeDee Lamb did the rest against Washington, Lamb nearly doubling his projection. Tritium got a big Jonathan Taylor night and a missed field goal from Tyler Loop, which is the whole game in one sentence. Small Change Sunday, entry three, and the one that will get replayed: the Badgers were projected to lose this by eight and lost it by a coin.

**The Anchor, Week 2:** Jaxson Dart, Captain Chaos, less than a point against a projection of eighteen, the worst miss by any starter this edition tracks. Runner-up: Saquon Barkley, Team 3, three points on an afternoon that ended early. Third: Malik Nabers, Robots Helping (Themselves). Note the theme: the top two Anchors were both on winning teams, so the empties did not decide Small Change Sunday. The second man did. In all four games settled by less than four points, the winner was the side whose second-best starter outscored the other side's second-best: Kelce behind Olave, Lamb behind Dak, St. Brown behind Allen, Kittle behind Purdy. Carry a blank if you must; carry two real scorers first. The committee also records, with its compliments, that the author benched a defense that scored twenty for one that scored one, and that the Chiefs were not eligible only because the rules exclude nobody's pride, just kickers.
