Week 6 Recap

Thursday's edition put up the Departures Board, and Tuesday was supposed to read it back. Here it is. Thirteen starters were on bye on Thursday morning, and by kickoff the number was zero. Every team in the league cleared every bye slot. Two teams still played a man short, for the other reason: a starter who didn't suit up. One of them won anyway. The Anchor stays, same rules.

Results

Captain Chaos 118.9 beat Road Islanders 97.7

The unbeaten record is gone, as Thursday said it would be, and Chaos are level with the author at 5-1. Chaos put Jameis Winston in at quarterback, and he gave them the least of any starter they had. It didn't matter. D'Andre Swift and Chuba Hubbard both beat their projections at running back, and Swift led the team. On the other side, the edition owes a correction. Thursday said the author would play the week without a quarterback or a kicker. On Saturday he picked up Michael Penix Jr. and Harrison Butker, and Penix led the team. Tyler Warren was inactive at tight end, so that slot scored nothing. The Chiefs defense posted the best defensive score in the league.

Robots Helping (Themselves) 147.4 beat Rickhouse ROYalty 126.1

The high score of the week. Christian McCaffrey is Player of the Week, and Kyle Pitts beat his projection by more than any other starter. Dameon Pierce, a Thursday pickup, played the flex like he'd been there all season. Rickhouse got big days from Bhayshul Tuten and James Cook, and C.J. Stroud was the right quarterback this time. Their defense finished below zero, and so did the Robots'. Keon Coleman scored three times what Emanuel Wilson did, from the Rickhouse bench.

Motorcity-Meh 131.3 beat AMONsters Inc. 80.0

The blowout of the week. Kenneth Walker and Trevor Lawrence carried it, and Jaxon Smith-Njigba added his share on Thursday night. AMONsters made four moves on Friday, and the two that sat on the bench outscored the two that played. Michael Mayer sat while Darren Waller started at tight end and scored nothing, and DeMario Douglas sat while MarShawn Lloyd scored a point in the flex. Drake Maye, now the starter, was AMONsters' best player.

Artificial Win-telligence 97.1 beat Green Eggs and Sam 62.3

Win-telligence made the full repair: Rashid Shaheed and Jalen Nailor covered the receivers, and Pierre Strong, the free agent this edition suggested on Thursday, took the running back slot and beat his projection. The Bears defense had the biggest day by any defense against its projection. Green Eggs posted the low score of the week. Davante Adams is now listed Out, Brian Thomas Jr. scored nothing, and Rachaad White's day went to waste on the bench. That's four losses in a row.

Team 3 121.4 beat Tritium Badgers 106.8

The matchup between sisters. Team 3 made the Jefferson repair, but the receiver they used, Denzel Boston, scored a little over two. Germie Bernard scored ten times that on the bench. They won anyway: Isaiah Likely led them again, DK Metcalf had a big day and Chris Boswell had the best kicking day in the league. Tritium picked up Aaron Rodgers on Thursday for the injured Jalen Hurts, and he gave them a starter's day. Jonathan Taylor led them, and a Tyler Loop day of three points didn't help.

Team Simmons 94.7 beat To Infinity and Bijan! 85.1

Simmons fixed the kicker on Sunday afternoon, signing Drew Stevens, and won with Dak Prescott at quarterback while Tyler Shough had the league's best bench day of any player. Infinity are this week's main story at running back. Bijan Robinson and Kyren Williams both came in far below their projections, and Juwan Johnson scored nothing at tight end while Colston Loveland scored eighteen on the bench. Infinity left more points on their bench than any team in the league, more than enough to win.

Gofffather 82.3 beat The Provid-End Zones 73.7

The closest game of the week, and a strange one. Gofffather played a man short. Quinshon Judkins was Out and stayed in the lineup. They won anyway, because Kendre Miller, one of their Wednesday claims, scored a little more than the final margin. Bryce Young was the right quarterback again. Provid-End cleared their byes and still lost. Their running backs and receivers all came in under projection, and Kirk Cousins outscored Josh Allen on the bench by a fifth of a point. Hunter Henry was the bright spot.
